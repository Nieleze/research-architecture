# Nieleze Assessment Schema

## Purpose

This document describes how Nieleze's analytical architecture can be applied through structured assessments.

The assessments translate the broader research architecture into a focused examination of organizational sensing, interpretation, escalation, intervention, coherence, and recovery.

## Assessment Logic

The underlying logic is:

**Condition → Signal → Interpretation → Escalation → Decision → Intervention → Outcome**

The assessment does not assume that this sequence is linear. Breakdowns may occur at any stage, and conditions at one stage may influence another through feedback and interaction.

## Structural Risk Snapshot

The Structural Risk Snapshot is an introductory assessment focused on emerging-risk visibility.

It examines questions such as:

- What emerging conditions are visible?
- What signals may be weak, suppressed, or overlooked?
- How are signals interpreted?
- What determines whether concerns are escalated?
- How much intervention time remains?
- Where might structural conditions allow risk to accumulate?

The purpose is to provide a concise view of potential structural risk before visible failure.

## Organizational Sensing Assessment

The Organizational Sensing Assessment examines how an organization detects, interprets, escalates, and responds to emerging conditions.

Relevant dimensions include:

- signal detection;
- ambiguity recognition;
- interpretive capacity;
- organizational sensing modes;
- escalation pathways;
- decision processes;
- information flow;
- oversight;
- feedback and learning.

The assessment is concerned not simply with whether information exists, but with whether the organization can convert meaningful signals into timely understanding and action.

## Organizational Coherence Map

The Organizational Coherence Map examines relationships among interpretation, coordination, responsibility, and action.

Relevant questions include:

- Do different parts of the organization construct compatible interpretations?
- Where does information become fragmented?
- Where are responsibilities unclear or disconnected?
- Where can coordination degrade?
- Where might organizational activity remain operational while structural coherence deteriorates?

## Assessment Dimensions

Across assessments, relevant dimensions may include:

| Dimension | Core question |
|---|---|
| Sensing | What are we noticing — and what are we missing? |
| Interpretation | What does the signal mean? |
| Escalation | When does the signal become actionable? |
| Decision | Can the organization decide effectively under uncertainty? |
| Intervention | Why does intervention arrive too late? |
| Trust & Oversight | Who is responsible for understanding and acting on what is seen? |
| Failure Dynamics | How might failure propagate? |
| Resilience & Adaptation | Can the system recover and learn? |
| Organizational Coherence | Can distributed actors maintain a sufficiently shared understanding for coordinated action? |

## Output

An assessment should identify:

1. observable signals or conditions;
2. structural mechanisms affecting those signals;
3. points of interpretive or organizational degradation;
4. escalation and intervention constraints;
5. potential failure or propagation pathways;
6. remaining recovery or adaptation capacity; and
7. areas where additional investigation may be warranted.

## Relationship to the Research Architecture

The assessments are applied expressions of the broader Nieleze analytical architecture.

They should therefore be understood as **applications of the architecture**, rather than as separate theoretical systems.
